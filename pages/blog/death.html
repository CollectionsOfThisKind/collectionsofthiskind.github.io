On Death

{% include quotes/anonymous/motionandtime.html %}1

The instance of death is not the end of being mortal, nor is it immortality: it is amortality; a shrinking of the personal scope of being alive to its singularity. When we stop our perception of motion, time stops, for without motion there is no time, there is only the absolute domination of the now.2

 

Notes:

1 Which is, clearly, influenced by Book IV of Aristotle's Physics.

2 In this I guess I'm close to Wittgenstein when he talks of death and eternity in the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, but whereas he sees the opportunity for the eternal in life, I see it in death – that death is experienced for all time, because time collapses to an experiential singularity. I imagine death to be like all those moments just before you jolt yourself into consciousness and think, "how have I been driving for the last five minutes; I don't remember a thing..."
