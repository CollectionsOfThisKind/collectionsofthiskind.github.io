The Lessons of Artificial Intelligence VII:
We kept it all in...

I don't think "Artificial Intelligence" is a helpful term for Large Language Models; it implies something external to humanity, whereas they seem to me to be, in part, the next stage in the very human technology of language:

  • When we first opened our mouths, we could convey knowledge (about ourselves; our environment; others) to others directly.
  • When we invented writing, we could reliably communicate to single others at distance and across time.
  • When we invented printing (and later the radio), we could communicate with many people at distance and across time.
  • When we invented the internet, we democratised distant communications.

But all these mechanisms (even hypertext) were broadly linear. The knowledge conveyed was a pre-determined narrative, or a linear exploration in a conversation. Large Language Models allow us to access knowledge non-linearly. While it may (currently, and rather unimaginatively) be resolved into a narrative, the underlying engagement with knowledge is profoundly different from previous technologies – it is the probabilistic pulling of knowledge from the mass of human endeavour, and what it finds there is the hidden knowledge greater than the sum of its linearities. I think we're only just beginning to understand the potential of this; limited, as we are, to our own epistemological ruts.
