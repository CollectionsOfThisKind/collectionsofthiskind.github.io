Three things: the school-trip pileup

Looking from the coach window,
as the spray threw rainbows into the sun
and the car jammed next to us on the bridge slowly compressed to the width of a bicycle1,2.

4 Mar 1985

 

Notes:

1 It was some time in the spring of 1985, and we were on our way back from a school trip on a coach when there was a 40 car pileup. I don't think I ever have, or want to, see anything like it again. There were cars shunting into each other, over each other, through each other, and we ended up up against a flyover railing, crushing the VW Beetle next to us. Unlikely though it sounds, not a single person died.

2 This also reminds me of the other accident I saw, aged eight, when a cyclist was knocked from his bike, in the days before helmets. My parents stopped our car to help, telling us children to wait in our seats. I'd just got my First Aid badge, so I waited two minutes before going to help. There was nothing I could do once I had pushed through the small crowd, but hide behind my mother's legs and peek at the casualty, before she sternly sent me back. Nothing I could do, in part because I had no real courage to deal with it – the thing they don't teach you in First Aid courses as a child; but also, fortunately, not because he was dead, but because he was fine; a little blood from a cut to his forehead aside.
