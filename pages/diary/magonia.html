Magonia

Looking out over the southern Lakes at the twilight's fall, it was as if another landscape hung suspended high above ours, high over the hills: a mirage of cloud, fae, matching the earthly one exactly, save that where there were, on the ground, cwms, there were icefalls, and where there were dales there were rivers of ice. I gazed at it until the night fell, following each ridge and peak, tracing them against their counterpart. As the light faded I wondered, was this landscape one from the past – the deep past – peopled by mammoth and painted on cave walls, or a future as yet unrealised1 .
